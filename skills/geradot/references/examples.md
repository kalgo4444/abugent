# Real-Life Examples

These examples show how the method applies to real situations.
Use the example closest to the user's situation as a model, but always adapt the questions
to the user's own circumstances.

| # | Situation | Direction of review |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Quitting a job in anger | "Drunk" → sober review |
| 2 | Declining a scholarship because it seems "safe" | Sober → "drunk" review |
| 3 | A business promise made at a wedding | "Drunk" → sober review |

---

## Example 1: Quitting a Job in Anger

**Situation:** "My manager criticized me in front of everyone. I'm writing my resignation right now
and leaving tomorrow!"

**State of origin:** "Drunk." Signs: anger, "right now," "tomorrow."

**Sober council:**
- Have you found another job?
- How many months of savings do you have?
- Is the problem this single incident, or something that has continued for months?
- Can the resignation be withdrawn once submitted?

**Verdict: Adopt with modifications.**
The wish to leave may be justified; the "drunk" state revealed this truth. However, the resignation
should not be submitted today. First, update your résumé and begin looking for a new position.
In addition, speak with your manager privately.

**The next day:** Write the resignation letter, but save it without sending it.
Reread it after one week and ask yourself, "Is this still what I think?"

## Example 2: Giving Up a Dream for a "Safe" Decision

**Situation:** "I received a scholarship to study abroad, but I did the math: I have a stable job here,
and my parents live nearby. Logically, I should stay, so I wrote a letter declining the offer."

**State of origin:** "Sober." Signs: "did the math," "logically." Fear is hidden behind the numbers.

**Drunk council:**
- What was your first feeling when the scholarship arrived?
- What would you do if you were not afraid?
- Ten years from now, which would you regret more: leaving or staying?
- Could you pause your job for one or two years and return afterward?

**Verdict: two possible outcomes.**
- If the "drunk" council gives a strong "yes," do not send the letter. First, ask your employer about
  a leave of absence or the possibility of returning. Also plan how you will support your parents.
- If it also says "no," the decision to stay has now been reviewed twice and can be accepted with
  confidence.

**The next day:** Keep the letter unsent overnight. Tomorrow, read both lists side by side.

## Example 3: A Business Promise Made at a Wedding

**Situation:** "Last night at a wedding, my old friend and I agreed to open a café together!
Tomorrow I'll sell my car and put in my share."

**State of origin:** "Drunk." Signs: festive mood, the warmth of an old friendship, "tomorrow."

**Sober council:**
- Is there a business plan, including rent and monthly costs?
- Who will be responsible for what?
- Will the agreement be put in writing?
- If the café closes, how will you manage without a car?
- Does your friend still feel the same way this morning?

**Verdict: Adopt with modifications.**
Do not abandon the idea, but do not sell the car. Within one month, prepare a business plan together
with your friend and formalize the agreement in writing. Begin with a small trial, such as a market
stall or a delivery service.

**The next day:** Call your friend in the morning and ask, "Does what we agreed last night still hold
now that we are sober?" This is exactly what the Persians did.
