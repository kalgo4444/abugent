# Source and Historical Context

Read this file when the user asks about the source, Herodotus, or the origin of the method,
or when a response would benefit from historical grounding.

## Herodotus, *Histories*, Book 1, Chapter 133

Herodotus (5th century BCE) describes the customs of the ancient Persians as follows:

- The Persians deliberated on their most important affairs while drunk.
- On the following day, when they were sober, the master of the house in which they had met
  presented the decision to them again.
- If the decision still seemed sound when they were sober, they adopted it. Otherwise, they abandoned it.
- Conversely, any decision first reached while sober was reconsidered while drunk.

## A Parallel Source

Tacitus, in *Germania* (Chapter 22), reports a similar custom among the ancient Germanic peoples.
They discussed reconciliation, marriage alliances, the choice of chiefs, and war during feasts,
because at such times people think openly and do not conceal their true feelings.
On the following day, they reviewed the matter with a clear mind.

## A Modern Interpretation

The essence of the method lies not in wine but in passing a decision through two different mental states.
Each state can see what the other one misses:

| State | Modern meaning | Strength | Weakness |
|---|---|---|---|
| **"Drunk"** | excitement, inspiration, desire, emotion, courage, free thinking | sees large opportunities; is not held back by fear; reveals genuine wishes | ignores risk and cost; forgets about tomorrow |
| **"Sober"** | cool reasoning, numbers, verification, caution | detects errors, costs, and risks | can be overly fearful; may let an opportunity pass in the name of "safety" |

### Alcohol-Free Equivalents

**To enter the "drunk" state:**
- daydream freely in the evening;
- have an inspiring conversation with friends;
- go for a walk;
- ask yourself, "What would I do if I were not afraid?"

**To enter the "sober" state:**
- sit somewhere quiet in the morning;
- work with paper and numbers;
- explain the decision to another person;
- reread it after a waiting period.
